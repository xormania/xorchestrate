## Fairness, ambiguity and limits

Baseline and Jev receive the same observations and candidate probes. Baseline rules were frozen before inspecting held-out packets and do not read the answer key, finding names or paths. However, the baseline is a deliberately simple lexical checklist: it often does not compare booleans, identities or event order and can mistake valid controls for failures. A Jev improvement over this comparator would not establish improvement over a capable invariant checker. Develop that stronger comparator on development families in a subsequent version; do not tune it on this run's held-out results.

Development families are F01/F02/F04/F07; F03/F05/F06/F08/F09/F10 are held out. Family grouping is preserved for controls and derivatives. Four independently constructed F07 dependency/entry variants are development transfer, not four new families. Some relationship pairs combine a development family with a held-out family, so their score measures relationship transfer, not two wholly unseen families. Several pair labels accept uncertainty because compact observations cannot prove shared implementation identity. The F02/F03 relationship is especially ambiguous and accepts four alternatives; its agreement score provides little discriminatory evidence and must not be emphasized.

These are human-selected minimal summaries with explicit candidate mechanisms. They test focused classification and test choice, not unsupervised discovery from unfamiliar raw traces. Separate raw retention makes the compression auditable but does not remove selection bias. Probe-option agreement alone is not utility. Only p4/p7 have prespecified executable probes; unsupported selections must remain unrun, and a shared probe executes once per selector rather than once per selecting case. No novel defect is required or established by the additional known-family variants.
